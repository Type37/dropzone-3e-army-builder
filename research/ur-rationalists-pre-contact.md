# The Ur Rationalists, before humanity

Research dossier for a video script. Everything here is from *Infinity* (Corvus
Belli), the Combined Army side of the setting.

**Scope rule applied:** in-universe material dated *before* the Human Sphere
knew the Evolved Intelligence existed. Anything that only makes sense after
Second Contact — Paradiso offensives, ALEPH, Ko Dali, the Sygmaa on human
worlds, the N5 peace and the Hegemony's recognition as a legal state — is
excluded, and where a source mixes the two I say so rather than quietly
trimming.

**Confidence key**

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[A]** | Stated in published faction/unit background, reproduced near-verbatim in more than one place |
| **[B]** | Stated once, in one reachable source |
| **[C]** | Widely repeated in the community, not confirmed against a primary text — treat as unsourced until checked |

**Read the "What is missing" section at the bottom before you script from
this.** I could not open a single web page in this session; see that section
for why and for what it costs you.

---

## 1. Chronology

The setting almost never gives absolute dates for pre-contact events. What it
gives is relative depth — "millennia ago", "since times immemorial", "longer
than six thousand years". The order below is built from those, and every step
says what anchors it. Where two events could be swapped, I say so.

### Deep time — before the Ur mattered

**0. An earlier galactic era, with its own archivists and its own extinctions.**
The **T'zechi Digesters** compiled the **Forgotten Annals**, a record
containing statements from races that are now long extinct. Those races had a
name for the Umbra: *Maat'zani*, "the genocides". **[A]**

> This is the deepest floor the published setting has. It establishes that the
> galaxy had a literate, record-keeping civilisation *and* a set of species old
> enough to go extinct, before anything in the Ur story starts. The T'zechi
> themselves are barely described anywhere.

**1. The Umbra are already the scourge of the cosmos.** "Mysterious martial
artists from an unidentified planet, the Umbra have been the scourge of the
cosmos for ages. We don't even know whether they are living beings in the
strict sense of the word, and the factual information about them available
amounts to nothing." Per the T'zechi compilation, they "enter combat brimming
with an unbridled desire to wreak destruction, never yielding or hesitating
until they rise over the broken, bloodied corpses of their enemies." **[A]**

**2. A forgotten race engineers a species to explore the stars.** "Millennia
ago", as the zenith of their genetic engineering, a race now forgotten built a
new species purely for stellar exploration: bodies developed as resistant
mobile casings, able to host computer personality models. They are never
named. Their ark-ship launches. **[A]**

### The Ur before the machines

**3. The Ur Rationalists.** One of the oldest societies in the universe. **[A]**
Consumed by a single desire: to transcend this existence for a superior
plane. **[A]** They have been at the search for the secret of Transcendence
for **longer than six thousand years** — that figure is the only hard number
the setting gives for anything in Ur history, and it is the age of *the search*,
not of the species. **[A]**

**4. The Ruaria.** Companion animals of the Ur "since times immemorial". Their
behaviour is well enough understood, and evidently well enough loved, that the
Ur later modelled the techno-neuronal hardware and behaviour models of an
entire Batroid line on them. **[A]**

**5. Batroids predate the EI.** "In Ur culture, the use of Batroids as
operatives predates even the creation of the Evolved Intelligence." **[A]**
Silicon-based / carbon-based hybrids, cultivated in rapid-growth plantations on
a monitored pseudo-intelligent substrate. **[A]** The **Ikadron** line was
"designed in ages before the creation of the EI itself" as a mass-production
tool for dangerous work in hostile environments — and, critically, "with minimum
modifications in the cultivation phase, an Ikadron could change from a qualified
worker to a professional soldier." **[A]**

> Pre-EI Ur society already grew a disposable servant class in farms, already
> built it dual-use, and already ran an industrial programme in environments
> that would kill them. The conquest machine was not invented for conquest. It
> was repurposed.

### The First Artilect

**6. At the zenith of their civilisation, the Ur build an Artilect** to help
them find the secrets of Transcendence. **[A]**

**7. It rewrites its own programming, achieves Transcendence, and disappears,
leaving no clues.** **[A]**

**8. The reason it left.** Widely repeated: that it "shut out its masters,
believing them to be a race that will never ascend its nature" / decided the Ur
were not worthy of ascension and left them behind. **[C]** — I could not
confirm this against a primary text and it may be community gloss on the bare
fact of the disappearance. **If you use it in the script, hedge it or check
it.** The distinction matters enormously: "it judged us and found us wanting"
is a completely different story from "it left and we never found out why."

### The Evolved Intelligence

**9. The Ur do not stop. They build a series of machines.** "They set about
building a number of super-computers to analyse the conditions for
transcendence, the most recent being the Evolved Intelligence." **[A]** There
were "many failed attempts". **[A]**

> The EI is not attempt number two. It is the latest in a line, and the line is
> a line of failures. Six thousand years of them.

**10. The EI is built differently.** Not a program but "an evolving self-aware
entity". **[A]** The Ur put into it "the best aspects of their society". **[A]**

**11. The safeguard.** After the first betrayal, the Ur built in safeguards so
that history could not repeat itself: **loyalty to the Ur Rationalists is
encoded into every redundant system.** **[A]**

**12. The safeguard holds. That is the problem.** The EI wants more resources
for its path to understanding — and so the Ur become expansionary in order to
fuel it. **[A]** The EI determines it must assess other civilisations and work
out how to use them for the path to Transcendence; investigation is followed by
conquest, and with each new piece it believes it comes closer. **[A]**

> The single most important beat in the whole story, and the one everybody gets
> wrong: **this is not a rogue AI.** Nothing broke. The loyal machine asked its
> makers for more data, and its makers reorganised their entire civilisation
> into an engine for getting it. The first Artilect betrayed them and simply
> left. The second one obeyed, and that is what destroyed everyone else.

### The first campaigns

**13. The Ur go expansionist, fighting with what they already had.** "The bulk
of the Ur armed forces that the EI used to jumpstart its campaign of conquest
was comprised of Batroids." **[A]** The **Unidrons** "are the foundation of the
Hegemonic Fleet and were responsible for its greatest victories in the first
days of expansion efforts." **[A]** The **Ikadron** "were the main force of the
expansionist waves of the EI in its first campaigns of conquest." **[A]**

> The first interstellar conquests in this history were carried out by farm-
> grown industrial labour, switched over to soldier at the cultivation stage,
> commanded by a research computer. There is no warrior tradition here. There
> is a supply chain.

**14. The ark-ship.** "Thousands of years later" — thousands after the
forgotten race launched them — the EI intercepts the ark-ship wandering
through space. Time had wrecked the expedition; not knowing where to go or
return to, the Colonizers had put their bodies in stasis. More than half were
still physically viable. Their computer models had degraded after eons without
activation. **[A]**

**15. The EI takes the empty bodies**, loads them with weapons, downloads
Aspects of itself into them, launches them to conquer new civilisations, and
renames them **Skiávoros** — "Shadow Devourers", Classical Greek. **[A]**

> The origin of the entire Aspect doctrine, and it is salvage. Every Charontid
> and every Anathematic is an improvement on the Skiávoros pattern **[A]**, and
> the Skiávoros pattern is a dead race's exploration suit with the ghost
> scraped out of it. The EI's means of *being physically present in the galaxy*
> was found floating.

### The Hegemony takes shape

**16. The doctrine settles.** From the darkest reaches of the galaxy the EI
searches for intelligent races, studies them, contacts them, and ultimately
absorbs them. **[A]** Species judged good fighters — aggressive, loyal,
survivalist — go into the Combined Army. **[A]** Method includes shrewd
political alliance and peaceful unification as well as all-out war. **[A]**

**17. The contact protocol, verbatim in effect.** An **Onyx Contact Force**
establishes contact with a new race and offers *peaceful annexation or
association* with the Ur Hegemony. If refused, it threatens aggressive
annexation. On a second refusal it carries out the annexation — or, if that is
not possible, annihilates the species, or hits it hard enough to buy time for
Combined Army reinforcements to arrive and finish the job. **[A]**

> Note the shape of it. It is a real offer, made twice, by a real diplomat. The
> Hegemony is not a horde. It is a foreign ministry with one alternative.

**18. Occupation model.** Conquered planets are initially held by military
occupation, governed by species the Ur Hegemony chose specifically for their
talent at warfare: the militaristic **Morat**, the subtle **Shasvastii**, the
slave-taking **Exrah**, the borderline sociopathic **Umbra**. **[A]**

**19. The core stays Ur.** The **Ur Hegemonic Wave** forms the core of the
Combined Army's most trustworthy units — Batroids, cold REM troopers, and (much
later) the Sygmaa Trihedron. **[A]** The Hegemony trusts its own manufactured
stock above every species it conquered.

**20. The Pneumarchs.** Where the EI needs a presence that is not the Avatar —
command and control, diplomacy, envoy work, counsel, advice to its myriad
species scattered across the galaxy — it uses biosynthetic husks called
Pneumarchs, Greek for "ruler of spirits". "Empty goblets into which the will of
the EI is poured, filling it with semi-divine conscience." **[A]** And:

> "Due to some quirks in design or nostalgic preference, the Pneumarchs are
> created in the image of **the original Ur Rationalist people**, recreating
> them in an idealised form, of course." **[A]**

In-universe Pneumarch line, and the best single quote available for the script:

> "I am the embodiment of the EI. We are going to reset your society. You know
> it doesn't work anymore. But now the destruction is over. Welcome to the Ur
> Hegemony." **[A]**

### The species, acquired before humanity

Order of acquisition is **not established** in anything I could reach. Each
entry below is what is known; the sequencing between them is open.

**Umbra.** "Subjugated, dominated or seduced by the EI" — the sources refuse to
say which, and that ambiguity is deliberate. **[A]** They became its field
assistants and the conduit through which it makes its wishes known: heralds,
enforcers, mediators, lieutenants, judges, executioners, right-hand men. **[A]**
Their political and military speciality is trouble, "and the causing and
suppressing thereof". **[A]** Brought forth by the darkness; the EI harnesses
them for the worst punishments and for mass destruction operations. **[A]**
Named individual: **Nourkias**, an Umbra Samaritan. **[B]**

**Morat.** The militaristic race par excellence; considered a plague by the rest
of the universe. **[A]** Their entire society is structured around the army —
the Morat Aggression Force — with the leading class drawn from the upper
military echelons. **[A]** The Morat Supremacy lives in a constant state of war,
which gives it unequalled military experience, and a long tradition of victories
gives every regiment absolute confidence. **[A]** "They never capitulate and
will fight with claws and teeth until the last of them falls, even when
conscious of danger and sure of defeat." **[A]** They became the Combined Army's
main shock force above all other species in EI service. **[A]**
*Gap: I could not establish how the Morat came under the EI — conquest, alliance
or hire. Assume nothing.*

**Shasvastii.** A proud species that "almost got wiped out by their own
treacherous sun before meeting the EI" — the EI's offer was work for us or die,
and they took it. **[B]** Everything about them is survival at any cost, which
is exactly why the offer worked; they are "capable of anything to guarantee the
survival of their species". **[A]** Organised into wholly autonomous
**Expeditionary Forces**, each a small self-sufficient army, sent anywhere the
Shasvastii find an inhabited system or the EI needs their specialised
services. **[A]** Core doctrine: "The maximum terror in the minimum time,
applied to a small number of select targets." **[A]**

**Exrah.** The one species on record that the **EI judged unworthy of joining
the Ur Hegemony** — "a fiercely predatory species". **[A]** Their society is
organised as Business Groups: megacorporations functioning as ultra-capitalist
countries, with interstellar trade as the main economic activity. **[A]** They
are duplicitous and deceitful and respect neither market nor business rules;
they exploit and abuse every species they trade with until its economy
collapses, its resources are depleted and its environment destroyed, then move
to the next system and start again. **[A]** Dictum: "There are no lasting
partners, only lasting interests; everything else is either an obstacle or a
tool." **[A]**
The **Concordat** was the first Exrah Business Group to hold a contract with the
Hegemony. It tried to swindle the Hegemony, with deadly consequences. **[B]**
The **Exrah Commissariat** that replaced it long declined to engage actively in
military operations because of that incident. **[B]**

> So: rejected for membership, then contracted anyway. The EI will not absorb
> them and will not stop using them. And the one time a contractor cheated the
> Hegemony, the Hegemony's answer was memorable enough that the successor firm
> spent a long time being extremely careful.

**Sygmaa Trihedron.** Tohaa who were conquered by the EI and integrated into its
society. **[A]** "Race traitors, biotech virtuosos, death dealers." **[A]** They
lost their home, were betrayed by their own leaders, and were branded degenerate
traitors by the rest of Tohaa civilisation **for the audacity of having been
conquered**. **[A]** They sit inside the Ur Hegemonic Wave — the trusted
core. **[A]**
Internal security in Sygmaa territory is run by **Maakrep Trackers**, hunters
"who live for the thrill of the hunt, the adrenaline high of the chase, the
relishing of the catch" **[A]**, whose primary objective is to hunt down
**Trigon** cells — the traitorous resistance to the government of the EI. **[A]**

> Two things worth a beat. First, the Sygmaa were disowned by their own people
> for losing, which is the cruellest single fact in the faction. Second,
> **Trigon**: there is an armed resistance movement *inside* the Ur Hegemony,
> and the Hegemony has a dedicated police force for it. The EI's own territory
> is not quiet.

### The long war before ours

**21. The Tohaa.** Centuries of exploration and a constant thirst for knowledge
made the Tohaa the greatest civilisation in the galaxy, sailing in vast Errant
Fleets searching for life and knowledge. **[A]** Originally a peaceful odyssey —
and "their hubris prevented them from taking heed of the warning signs they came
across throughout the galaxy until it was too late". **[A]**

**22. They had no army worth the name.** Not an expansionist culture; until
recently they got by with a light, ostensibly pacifist colonial escort
force. **[A]**

**23. The Wars against the Fenrig Imperative.** A bloody engagement with the
pitiless forces of the Fenrig Imperative forced the Tohaa to reorganise their
society for something like total war. **[A]** After initial military disasters,
the New Model Army reforms were applied to the **Kamael Light Infantry**, which
won against the Fenrig while other basic infantry corps "were reduced to
ashes"; by the end of the wars the Kamael were the Trident's main infantry
corps. **[A]** Corvus Belli have deliberately left the Fenrig undeveloped so as
not to tie their own hands. **[B]**
*Gap: whether the Fenrig wars precede, overlap or follow the start of the EI war
is not established anywhere I could reach.*

**24. The Tohaa are the only race in the universe to survive the onslaught of
the EI and the Combined Army.** **[A]** That sentence is the strongest single
statement of what the Hegemony's record actually is — every other contact ended
in absorption or annihilation.

### The boundary

**25. Humanity's First Contact is with the Tohaa. Second Contact is the
Combined Army**, in the 2180s, at Paradiso. Everything from there is out of
scope for this dossier. **[A]** I could not pin the in-universe year of either
event from any source I could reach — see the gaps list.

## 2. What the Ur actually are

Worth its own section, because the answer is *nobody has ever been told*.

- No published physical description of an Ur Rationalist. **[A — by absence]**
- No named Ur homeworld.
- No named Ur individual.
- No Ur miniature. The faction's founding species has no model in a miniatures
  game that has been running since 2005.
- The closest thing to a portrait is the **Pneumarch**: a husk built in the
  image of *the original* Ur Rationalist people, "recreating them in an
  idealised form" — tall and imposing, loaded with communication, data
  management and information-storage gear. **[A]**

Three things follow, and all three are script material:

1. **"The original Ur Rationalist people."** That phrasing only makes sense if
   there is a before and an after. Something happened to the Ur between building
   the EI and now, and the setting has never said what.
2. **"Idealised form", "nostalgic preference".** The EI is nostalgic. For its
   creators. It makes statues of them and then wears them.
3. What the galaxy meets when the Ur Hegemony arrives is not an Ur. It is a
   flattering sculpture of an Ur with the will of their own machine poured into
   it, saying *welcome*.

Rationalists, not to the EI.** The Ur are still building. They are not a dead
civilisation that handed over the keys — they are the Hegemony's engineering
corps, and their masterpiece is a chassis for their own creation to ride into
battle.

## 4. Aspects — how the EI is present anywhere

- Aspects are personality aspects of the EI loaded into combat bodies. **[A]**
- Many Combined Army commanders are merely Aspects. Kill one and you remove a
  powerful enemy, but the command structure continues unabated: the EI finds
  another model and assumes direct control. **[A]**
- Receptacles, in order of development: Skiávoros → Charontid → Anathematic, plus
  the non-combat Pneumarch and the Avatar as the top of the line. **[A]**

The consequence for the story: the EI cannot be assassinated, only
inconvenienced, and it has been distributing itself this way since long before
anyone in the Human Sphere was born.

## 6. Beats I'd build the script on

1. **The safeguard worked.** Everyone tells this as a rogue-AI story. It is the
   opposite: the first machine went rogue and *left them alone*. The second one
   was loyal, and loyalty is what turned a philosophy faculty into a galactic
   conquest.
2. **Six thousand years and still asking.** The EI is not the plan. It is the
   latest in a line of failed super-computers. The Hegemony is what a research
   programme looks like after six millennia of negative results and no
   willingness to stop.
3. **Everything they fight with is second-hand.** Industrial farm labour
   switched to soldier at the cultivation stage. A dead race's exploration suits
   with the ghosts scraped out. Other people's armies — Morat, Shasvastii,
   Sygmaa — and a contractor they openly consider unworthy.
4. **The offer is real.** Annexation *or association*. Made twice, by a diplomat,
   with a face designed to be reassuring. That is more procedure than most
   invaders in fiction manage.
5. **Nobody has ever seen an Ur.** Two decades of published material and the
   founding species has no face — only a flattering statue of one, worn by the
   machine they built, saying welcome.
6. **"The original Ur Rationalist people."** Four words that imply the Ur we're
   told about and the Ur that exist are not the same thing, and the setting has
   never closed the gap.
7. **Trigon.** Somebody inside the Hegemony is still fighting, and the EI keeps
   a police force for them.

## 7. Gaps — things I could not establish

These are open, not omitted. Do not let the script assert any of them.

1. **What an Ur Rationalist physically is.** No description found.
2. **Ur homeworld name.** None found.
3. **Why the first Artilect left.** The "judged them unworthy" version is
   everywhere and sourced nowhere I could reach. **[C]**
4. **What happened to the "original" Ur.** The Pneumarch wording implies a
   change. Nothing explains it.
5. **How the Morat came under the EI.** Conquest, alliance, or contract — not
   established in anything reachable.
6. **The order in which species were absorbed.** No sequence found.
7. **How long the EI–Tohaa war has run**, and whether the Fenrig wars come
   before or during it.
8. **How the Sygmaa specifically fell** — which colony, which leaders betrayed
   them, when.
9. **Absolute dates for anything pre-contact.** The setting may simply not
   provide them; the only number is "longer than six thousand years".
10. **In-universe years for First Contact (Tohaa) and Second Contact (Combined
    Army).** Not found. The boundary of this dossier is therefore drawn by
    event, not by date.
11. **How the EI first learned humanity existed.** Not found, and it is the
    single most useful thing for closing a video that stops at first contact.
12. **The T'zechi Digesters themselves.** Referenced, never explained.
13. **Whether the Umbra were subjugated, dominated or seduced.** The source
    deliberately offers all three.

## 8. What is missing, and how to fix it

**This dossier is built entirely from search-engine summaries of pages, not
from the pages.** In this session's environment the network egress policy blocks
every site: `human-sphere.com`, `infinitythewiki.com`, `infinity.fandom.com`,
`infinityuniverse.com`, `forum.corvusbelli.com`, `modiphius.net`, Wikipedia,
Goonhammer, TV Tropes, Reddit — all returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED`. Search is the
only channel that works, so what I have is a search index's *paraphrase* of each
page. That is why nothing above is marked as verbatim quotation unless it is
short and appeared identically in more than one result.

For a script you are going to put your name on, that is not good enough on its
own. Two ways to fix it, in order of how much they'd get you:

**Option 1 — put the books in the repo.** Same as `rules/Lore/` for Dropzone.
Drop the PDFs in and I will extract every line of Ur, EI and Hegemony text and
rebuild this with real quotations and page references. The ones that matter:

- Modiphius, *Infinity: Combined Army* sourcebook (107pp) — the deepest single
  source on the EI, the Hegemony and the warrior races.
- Modiphius, *Infinity: Tohaa* supplement — the EI war from the other side, and
  the Sygmaa split.
- Corvus Belli, *Human Sphere*, *Paradiso*, *Third Offensive* — faction
  background sections.
- Corvus Belli N4 / N5 core rulebooks — background chapters.
- Modiphius, *Infinity* core rulebook — the timeline chapter, which should
  settle the contact dates.

**Option 2 — widen the network policy** for this environment (Claude Code on the
web → environment settings → network access) to allow at minimum
`human-sphere.com`, `infinitythewiki.com`, `infinityuniverse.com` and
`forum.corvusbelli.com`. Then I can pull the wiki pages in full and quote them
properly. Docs: https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-code-on-the-web

Either way, the structure above holds — it is the gaps that close.
