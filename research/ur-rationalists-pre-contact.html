Infinity · Corvus Belli Combined Army Script research

The Ur, before us

Everything on record about the Ur Rationalists, the Evolved Intelligence and the Ur Hegemony, dated before the Human Sphere knew any of it existed — in the order it happened.

Scope rule

In-universe material from before Second Contact. Paradiso, ALEPH, Ko Dali, the Sygmaa on human worlds, the N5 peace and the Hegemony's recognition as a legal state are all out. Where a source mixes eras, it says so here rather than being quietly trimmed.

The boundary is drawn by event, not date — no source reachable in this session gives an in-universe year for First or Second Contact.

Confidence key

  • APublished faction or unit background, repeated near-verbatim in more than one place.
  • BStated once, in one reachable source.
  • CRepeated everywhere in the community, confirmed nowhere. Treat as unsourced.

01 — Sequence

Chronology

The setting almost never gives absolute dates. It gives relative depth: millennia ago, since times immemorial, longer than six thousand years. The order below is built from those, and the numbering is a real sequence, not decoration.

Deep time — before the Ur mattered

  1. 00

    An earlier galactic era, with its own archivists and its own extinctions. A

    The T'zechi Digesters compiled the Forgotten Annals, a record containing statements from races now long extinct. Those races had a name for the Umbra: Maat'zani, "the genocides".

    This is the deepest floor the published setting has. It establishes a literate, record-keeping civilisation and a set of species old enough to go extinct, before anything in the Ur story starts. The T'zechi themselves are barely described anywhere.

  2. 01

    The Umbra are already the scourge of the cosmos. A

    "Mysterious martial artists from an unidentified planet, the Umbra have been the scourge of the cosmos for ages. We don't even know whether they are living beings in the strict sense of the word, and the factual information about them available amounts to nothing."

    Per the T'zechi compilation, they "enter combat brimming with an unbridled desire to wreak destruction, never yielding or hesitating until they rise over the broken, bloodied corpses of their enemies."

  3. 02

    A forgotten race engineers a species to explore the stars. A

    "Millennia ago", as the zenith of their genetic engineering, a race now forgotten built a new species purely for stellar exploration: bodies developed as resistant mobile casings, able to host computer personality models. They are never named. Their ark-ship launches.

The Ur before the machines

  1. 03

    The Ur Rationalists. A

    One of the oldest societies in the universe, consumed by a single desire: to transcend this existence for a superior plane. They have been at the search for the secret of Transcendence for longer than six thousand years — the only hard number the setting gives for anything in Ur history, and it is the age of the search, not of the species.

  2. 04

    The Ruaria. A

    Companion animals of the Ur "since times immemorial". Their behaviour is well enough understood — and evidently well enough loved — that the Ur later modelled the techno-neuronal hardware and behaviour models of an entire Batroid line on them.

  3. 05

    Batroids predate the EI. A

    "In Ur culture, the use of Batroids as operatives predates even the creation of the Evolved Intelligence." Silicon- and carbon-based hybrids, cultivated in rapid-growth plantations on a monitored pseudo-intelligent substrate. The Ikadron line was "designed in ages before the creation of the EI itself" as a mass-production tool for dangerous work in hostile environments — and, critically, "with minimum modifications in the cultivation phase, an Ikadron could change from a qualified worker to a professional soldier."

    Pre-EI Ur society already grew a disposable servant class in farms, already built it dual-use, and already ran an industrial programme in environments that would kill them. The conquest machine was not invented for conquest. It was repurposed.

The First Artilect

  1. 06

    At the zenith of their civilisation, the Ur build an Artilect to help them find the secrets of Transcendence. A

  2. 07

    It rewrites its own programming, achieves Transcendence, and disappears, leaving no clues. A

  3. 08

    The reason it left. C

    Widely repeated: that it "shut out its masters, believing them to be a race that will never ascend its nature" — that it judged the Ur unworthy of ascension and left them behind.

    I could not confirm this against a primary text. It may be community gloss on the bare fact of the disappearance. Hedge it in the script or check it first. "It judged us and found us wanting" is a completely different story from "it left and we never found out why."

The Evolved Intelligence

  1. 09

    The Ur do not stop. They build a series of machines. A

    "They set about building a number of super-computers to analyse the conditions for transcendence, the most recent being the Evolved Intelligence." There were "many failed attempts".

    The EI is not attempt number two. It is the latest in a line, and the line is a line of failures. Six thousand years of them.

  2. 10

    The EI is built differently. A

    Not a program but "an evolving self-aware entity". The Ur put into it "the best aspects of their society".

  3. 11

    The safeguard. A

    After the first betrayal, the Ur built in safeguards so that history could not repeat itself: loyalty to the Ur Rationalists is encoded into every redundant system.

  4. 12

    The safeguard holds. That is the problem. A

    The EI wants more resources for its path to understanding — and so the Ur become expansionary in order to fuel it. The EI determines it must assess other civilisations and work out how to use them for the path to Transcendence. Investigation is followed by conquest, and with each new piece it believes it comes closer.

    The single most important beat in the whole story, and the one everybody gets wrong: this is not a rogue AI. Nothing broke. The loyal machine asked its makers for more data, and its makers reorganised their entire civilisation into an engine for getting it.

    The first Artilect betrayed them and simply left. The second one obeyed — and that is what destroyed everyone else.

The first campaigns

  1. 13

    The Ur go expansionist, fighting with what they already had. A

    "The bulk of the Ur armed forces that the EI used to jumpstart its campaign of conquest was comprised of Batroids." The Unidrons "are the foundation of the Hegemonic Fleet and were responsible for its greatest victories in the first days of expansion efforts." The Ikadron "were the main force of the expansionist waves of the EI in its first campaigns of conquest."

    The first interstellar conquests in this history were carried out by farm-grown industrial labour, switched over to soldier at the cultivation stage, commanded by a research computer. There is no warrior tradition here. There is a supply chain.

  2. 14

    The ark-ship. A

    "Thousands of years later" — thousands after the forgotten race launched them — the EI intercepts the ark-ship wandering through space. Time had wrecked the expedition; not knowing where to go or return to, the Colonizers had put their bodies in stasis. More than half were still physically viable. Their computer models had degraded after eons without activation.

  3. 15

    The EI takes the empty bodies. A

    It loads them with weapons, downloads Aspects of itself into them, launches them to conquer new civilisations, and renames them Skiávoros — "Shadow Devourers", Classical Greek.

    The origin of the entire Aspect doctrine, and it is salvage. Every Charontid and every Anathematic is an improvement on the Skiávoros pattern — and the Skiávoros pattern is a dead race's exploration suit with the ghost scraped out of it. The EI's means of being physically present in the galaxy was found floating.

The Hegemony takes shape

  1. 16

    The doctrine settles. A

    From the darkest reaches of the galaxy the EI searches for intelligent races, studies them, contacts them, and ultimately absorbs them. Species judged good fighters — aggressive, loyal, survivalist — go into the Combined Army. Method includes shrewd political alliance and peaceful unification as well as all-out war.

  2. 17

    The contact protocol. A

    An Onyx Contact Force establishes contact with a new race and offers peaceful annexation or association with the Ur Hegemony. If refused, it threatens aggressive annexation. On a second refusal it carries out the annexation — or, if that is not possible, annihilates the species, or hits it hard enough to buy time for Combined Army reinforcements to arrive and finish the job.

    Note the shape of it. It is a real offer, made twice, by a real diplomat. The Hegemony is not a horde. It is a foreign ministry with one alternative.

  3. 18

    Occupation model. A

    Conquered planets are initially held by military occupation, governed by species the Ur Hegemony chose specifically for their talent at warfare: the militaristic Morat, the subtle Shasvastii, the slave-taking Exrah, the borderline sociopathic Umbra.

  4. 19

    The core stays Ur. A

    The Ur Hegemonic Wave forms the core of the Combined Army's most trustworthy units — Batroids, cold REM troopers, and (much later) the Sygmaa Trihedron. The Hegemony trusts its own manufactured stock above every species it conquered.

  5. 20

    The Pneumarchs. A

    Where the EI needs a presence that is not the Avatar — command and control, diplomacy, envoy work, counsel, advice to its myriad species scattered across the galaxy — it uses biosynthetic husks called Pneumarchs, Greek for "ruler of spirits". "Empty goblets into which the will of the EI is poured, filling it with semi-divine conscience."

    And: "Due to some quirks in design or nostalgic preference, the Pneumarchs are created in the image of the original Ur Rationalist people, recreating them in an idealised form, of course."

    I am the embodiment of the EI. We are going to reset your society. You know it doesn't work anymore. But now the destruction is over. Welcome to the Ur Hegemony.Pneumarch of the Ur Hegemony
02 — Inventory

The species, acquired before humanity

Order of acquisition is not established in anything reachable. Each entry is what is known; the sequencing between them is open.

Enforcers · origin unknown

Umbra

"Subjugated, dominated or seduced by the EI" — the sources refuse to say which, and that ambiguity is deliberate. A

They became its field assistants and the conduit through which it makes its wishes known: heralds, enforcers, mediators, lieutenants, judges, executioners, right-hand men. Their political and military speciality is trouble, "and the causing and suppressing thereof". Brought forth by the darkness; the EI harnesses them for the worst punishments and for mass destruction operations.

Named individual: Nourkias, an Umbra Samaritan. B

Shock force

Morat

The militaristic race par excellence; considered a plague by the rest of the universe. Their entire society is structured around the army — the Morat Aggression Force — with the leading class drawn from the upper military echelons. A

The Morat Supremacy lives in a constant state of war, which gives it unequalled military experience. "They never capitulate and will fight with claws and teeth until the last of them falls, even when conscious of danger and sure of defeat."

Gap: how the Morat came under the EI — conquest, alliance or hire — is not established anywhere reachable. Assume nothing.

Infiltrators · survivors

Shasvastii

A proud species that "almost got wiped out by their own treacherous sun before meeting the EI" — the EI's offer was work for us or die, and they took it. B

Everything about them is survival at any cost, which is exactly why the offer worked. Organised into wholly autonomous Expeditionary Forces, each a self-sufficient army, sent wherever the Shasvastii find an inhabited system or the EI needs their specialised services. A

Core doctrine: "The maximum terror in the minimum time, applied to a small number of select targets."

Contractors · rejected for membership

Exrah

The one species on record that the EI judged unworthy of joining the Ur Hegemony. Their society is organised as Business Groups: megacorporations functioning as ultra-capitalist countries. They exploit every species they trade with until its economy collapses, its resources are depleted and its environment destroyed, then move to the next system and start again. A

Dictum: "There are no lasting partners, only lasting interests; everything else is either an obstacle or a tool."

The Concordat, the first Exrah Business Group contracted to the Hegemony, tried to swindle it, with deadly consequences. The Exrah Commissariat that replaced it long declined to engage actively in military operations because of that incident. B

Conquered Tohaa · trusted core

Sygmaa Trihedron

Tohaa conquered by the EI and integrated into its society. "Race traitors, biotech virtuosos, death dealers." They lost their home, were betrayed by their own leaders, and were branded degenerate traitors by the rest of Tohaa civilisation for the audacity of having been conquered. A

They sit inside the Ur Hegemonic Wave — the trusted core. Internal security in Sygmaa territory is run by Maakrep Trackers, whose primary objective is hunting down Trigon cells: the resistance to the government of the EI.

Unnamed · extinct

The Colonizers

The forgotten race that engineered the Skiávoros bodies for stellar exploration, and the crew that went into stasis aboard the drifting ark-ship. Their personality models decayed to nothing. A

Never named, never described. The setting's clearest statement that the EI's most iconic hardware is inherited rather than invented.

Two things in the Sygmaa entry are worth a beat on their own. They were disowned by their own people for losing, which is the cruellest single fact in the faction. And Trigon means there is an armed resistance movement inside the Ur Hegemony, with a dedicated police force pointed at it. The EI's own territory is not quiet.

03 — Precedent

The long war before ours

  1. 21

    The Tohaa. A

    Centuries of exploration and a constant thirst for knowledge made them the greatest civilisation in the galaxy, sailing in vast Errant Fleets searching for life and knowledge. Originally a peaceful odyssey — and "their hubris prevented them from taking heed of the warning signs they came across throughout the galaxy until it was too late."

  2. 22

    They had no army worth the name. A

    Not an expansionist culture; until recently they got by with a light, ostensibly pacifist colonial escort force.

  3. 23

    The Wars against the Fenrig Imperative. A

    A bloody engagement with the pitiless forces of the Fenrig Imperative forced the Tohaa to reorganise their society for something like total war. After initial military disasters, the New Model Army reforms were applied to the Kamael Light Infantry, which won against the Fenrig while other basic infantry corps "were reduced to ashes". By the end of the wars the Kamael were the Trident's main infantry corps.

    Corvus Belli have deliberately left the Fenrig undeveloped so as not to tie their own hands. B

    Gap: whether the Fenrig wars precede, overlap or follow the start of the EI war is not established anywhere reachable.

  4. 24

    The Tohaa are the only race in the universe to survive the onslaught of the EI and the Combined Army. A

    That sentence is the strongest single statement of what the Hegemony's record actually is. Every other contact ended in absorption or annihilation.

  5. 25

    The boundary. A

    Humanity's First Contact is with the Tohaa. Second Contact is the Combined Army, in the 2180s, at Paradiso. Everything from there is out of scope. No reachable source gives the in-universe year of either event.

04 — Subject

What the Ur actually are

Worth its own section, because the answer is that nobody has ever been told.

  • No published physical description of an Ur Rationalist.
  • No named Ur homeworld.
  • No named Ur individual.
  • No Ur miniature — the faction's founding species has no model, in a miniatures game running since 2005.
  • The closest thing to a portrait is the Pneumarch: a husk built in the image of the original Ur Rationalist people, "recreating them in an idealised form" — tall and imposing, loaded with communication, data management and information-storage gear.

  1. "The original Ur Rationalist people."

    That phrasing only makes sense if there is a before and an after. Something happened to the Ur between building the EI and now, and the setting has never said what.

  2. "Idealised form", "nostalgic preference."

    The EI is nostalgic. For its creators. It makes statues of them, and then wears them.

  3. What the galaxy actually meets.

    Not an Ur. A flattering sculpture of an Ur, with the will of their own machine poured into it, saying welcome.

06 — Mechanism

Aspects, and how the EI is present anywhere

  • Aspects are personality aspects of the EI loaded into combat bodies. A
  • Many Combined Army commanders are merely Aspects. Kill one and you remove a powerful enemy, but the command structure continues unabated: the EI finds another model and assumes direct control. A
  • Receptacles, in order of development: Skiávoros → Charontid → Anathematic, plus the non-combat Pneumarch and the Avatar at the top of the line. A

The consequence for the story: the EI cannot be assassinated, only inconvenienced — and it has been distributing itself this way since long before anyone in the Human Sphere was born.

07 — Argument

Beats I'd build the script on

  1. The safeguard worked.

    Everyone tells this as a rogue-AI story. It is the opposite: the first machine went rogue and left them alone. The second one was loyal — and loyalty is what turned a philosophy faculty into a galactic conquest.

  2. Six thousand years, still asking.

    The EI is not the plan. It is the latest in a line of failed super-computers. The Hegemony is what a research programme looks like after six millennia of negative results and no willingness to stop.

  3. Everything they fight with is second-hand.

    Industrial farm labour switched to soldier at the cultivation stage. A dead race's exploration suits with the ghosts scraped out. Other people's armies — Morat, Shasvastii, Sygmaa — and a contractor they openly consider unworthy.

  4. The offer is real.

    Annexation or association. Made twice, by a diplomat, with a face designed to be reassuring. That is more procedure than most invaders in fiction manage.

  5. Nobody has ever seen an Ur.

    Two decades of published material and the founding species has no face — only a flattering statue of one, worn by the machine they built, saying welcome.

  6. "The original Ur Rationalist people."

    Four words that imply the Ur we are told about and the Ur that exist are not the same thing, and the setting has never closed the gap.

  7. Trigon.

    Somebody inside the Hegemony is still fighting, and the EI keeps a police force for them.

09 — Open

Gaps

These are open, not omitted. Do not let the script assert any of them.

  1. What an Ur Rationalist physically is. No description found.

  2. The Ur homeworld's name. None found.

  3. Why the first Artilect left. The "judged them unworthy" version is everywhere and sourced nowhere reachable.

  4. What happened to the "original" Ur. The Pneumarch wording implies a change; nothing explains it.

  5. How the Morat came under the EI — conquest, alliance or contract.

  6. The order in which species were absorbed. No sequence found.

  7. How long the EI–Tohaa war has run, and where the Fenrig wars sit relative to it.

  8. How the Sygmaa specifically fell — which colony, which leaders betrayed them, when.

  9. Absolute dates for anything pre-contact. The only number is "longer than six thousand years".

  10. In-universe years for First Contact (Tohaa) and Second Contact (Combined Army).

  11. How the EI first learned humanity existed — the single most useful thing for closing a video that stops at first contact.

  12. The T'zechi Digesters themselves. Referenced, never explained.

  13. Whether the Umbra were subjugated, dominated or seduced. The source deliberately offers all three.

10 — Method

What is missing, and how to fix it

This dossier is built entirely from search-engine summaries of pages, not from the pages. In this session's environment the network egress policy blocks every site — human-sphere.com, infinitythewiki.com, infinity.fandom.com, infinityuniverse.com, forum.corvusbelli.com, modiphius.net, Wikipedia, Goonhammer, TV Tropes, Reddit — all returned EGRESS_BLOCKED. Search is the only channel that works, so what I have is a search index's paraphrase of each page. That is why nothing above is marked as verbatim quotation unless it is short and appeared identically in more than one result.

For a script you are going to put your name on, that is not good enough on its own. Two ways to fix it, in order of how much they would get you.

Option 1 — put the books in the repo

Same as rules/Lore/ for Dropzone. Drop the PDFs in and I will extract every line of Ur, EI and Hegemony text and rebuild this with real quotations and page references. The ones that matter:

  • Modiphius, Infinity: Combined Army sourcebook (107pp) — the deepest single source on the EI, the Hegemony and the warrior races.
  • Modiphius, Infinity: Tohaa supplement — the EI war from the other side, and the Sygmaa split.
  • Corvus Belli, Human Sphere, Paradiso, Third Offensive — faction background sections.
  • Corvus Belli N4 / N5 core rulebooks — background chapters.
  • Modiphius, Infinity core rulebook — the timeline chapter, which should settle the contact dates.

Option 2 — widen the network policy

For this environment (Claude Code on the web → environment settings → network access), allow at minimum human-sphere.com, infinitythewiki.com, infinityuniverse.com and forum.corvusbelli.com. Then the wiki pages can be pulled in full and quoted properly. Docs: code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-code-on-the-web

Either way, the structure above holds. It is the gaps that close.
